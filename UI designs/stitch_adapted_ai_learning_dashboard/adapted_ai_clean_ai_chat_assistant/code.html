Can you explain why scaled dot-product attention divides by the square root of d_k, and how that relates to softmax gradients?
neurology
Adapted Tutor 10:25 AM

When the key vector dimension d_k is large, the magnitude of the dot products grows substantially. Larger values push the standard softmax function into regions where gradients are extremely tiny (vanishing gradient problem), slowing or stalling training.

Assuming independent components with zero mean and unit variance, their dot product has mean 0 and variance d_k. Dividing by √d_k scales the variance strictly back to 1.

Canonical Formula
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax((Q K^T) / √d_k) · V
